GLOSSARY & FORMULAE

Complete reference of all acoustic terms, symbols, physical models and equations used in the SONIQ prediction engine. All formulations follow ICAO Annex 16 Vol. I conventions.

FUNDAMENTAL ACOUSTIC QUANTITIES
SPL SOUND PRESSURE LEVEL
Sound Pressure Level
The logarithmic ratio of the RMS sound pressure to a reference pressure. The primary metric for noise measurement and regulation.
SPL = 20 · log₁₀(p_rms / p_ref)
p_ref = 20 µPa (threshold of hearing)
dB re 20 µPa
dB(A) A-WEIGHTED SPL
A-Weighted Sound Level
SPL corrected by the A-weighting curve, which approximates human ear sensitivity as a function of frequency. Required for all community noise assessments.
SPL_A(f) = SPL(f) + W_A(f)
W_A: IEC 61672 standardised weighting curve
e.g. W_A(1000Hz)=0, W_A(63Hz)=−26.2 dB
dB(A)
OASPL OVERALL SPL
Overall Sound Pressure Level
Energy sum of SPL across all frequency bands. Used when a single broadband number is required.
OASPL = 10 · log₁₀(Σ 10^(SPLᵢ/10))
Sum over all 1/3-octave or octave bands
dB or dB(A)
EPNL EFFECTIVE PERCEIVED NOISE LEVEL
Effective Perceived Noise Level
ICAO certification metric that combines peak noise, duration, and tonality corrections for a flyover event. The primary type-certification standard (Annex 16).
EPNL = PNLTM + 10·log₁₀(∫ 10^(PNLT/10) dt) − 13
PNLTM = max tone-corrected perceived noise
Duration integral normalised to T₀ = 10 s
EPNdB
p₀, ρ₀, c₀ AMBIENT PROPERTIES
Atmospheric Reference Values
Standard sea-level atmosphere values used throughout the acoustic model. Density varies with altitude using ISA standard lapse rate.
ρ₀ = 1.225 · exp(−h/8500) [kg/m³]
c₀ = 340 m/s (ISA SL)
P₀ = 101 325 Pa
h = geometric altitude [m]
kg/m³ · m/s · Pa
r OBSERVER DISTANCE
Geometric Spreading
In free-field propagation, sound level decreases with the square of distance (inverse square law), resulting in −6 dB per doubling of distance.
ΔL = −20 · log₁₀(r₂/r₁)
Point source: −6 dB per distance doubling
Line source: −3 dB per distance doubling
dB / doubling
ROTOR GEOMETRY & OPERATING PARAMETERS
BPF BLADE PASSAGE FREQUENCY
Blade Passage Frequency
The fundamental tonal frequency produced by a rotating rotor. Each blade passage creates a pressure pulse at the observer. Dominates the low-frequency noise spectrum of multirotor eVTOL.
BPF = B · n [Hz]
B = number of blades per rotor
n = rotational speed [rev/s] = RPM/60
Harmonics: 2×BPF, 3×BPF, …, n×BPF
Hz
Vₜ TIP SPEED
Blade Tip Speed
The velocity of the blade tip. Critical acoustic parameter — both thickness and broadband noise scale strongly with tip speed (M⁴ to M⁵). Reducing tip speed is the most effective single noise mitigation.
V_tip = Ω · R [m/s]
M_tip = V_tip / c₀
Ω = 2π·RPM/60 [rad/s]
R = rotor radius [m]
m/s | M
DL DISC LOADING
Disc Loading
Thrust force per unit rotor disc area. Higher disc loading requires greater induced velocity, increasing loading noise. Low disc loading is acoustically advantageous but increases rotor size.
DL = T / A_disc [N/m²]
A_disc = π · R²
Induced velocity: v_i = √(T/(2ρ₀·A_disc))
N/m²
μ ADVANCE RATIO
Advance Ratio
Ratio of forward flight speed to blade tip speed. Governs blade-vortex interaction (BVI) probability. Critical parameter for transition-phase noise in lift+cruise configurations.
μ = V_cruise / V_tip
μ < 0.05: hover-like, low BVI risk
0.05 < μ < 0.15: BVI peak region
μ > 0.2: cruise, BVI diminishes
dimensionless
ACOUSTIC NOISE MODELS
L_th THICKNESS NOISE
Thickness Noise — Lowson (1970) / Farassat 1A
Noise generated by blade volume displacing air. Scales with Mach² and blade volume. Produces strong harmonics at BPF. Dominant at high tip speeds. Directional — strongest in the rotor plane.
L_thick ≈ 20·log₁₀(ρ₀·c₀·B·Ω²·R²·c·(t/c) / (4π·r))
+ 20·log₁₀(M_tip) + 10·log₁₀(N)
c = chord, t/c = thickness ratio ≈ 0.12
N = number of rotors
dB re 20 µPa
L_ld LOADING NOISE
Loading Noise — Ffowcs Williams & Hawkings
Noise from fluctuating aerodynamic lift and drag forces on blades. Dominant at low tip speeds. Scales with thrust and blade passage frequency. Primary driver of low-frequency tonal content.
L_load ≈ 20·log₁₀(B·dT/dt / (4π·c₀·ρ₀·r²)) + 94
+ 10·log₁₀(N)
dT/dt = T·B·Ω·φ_phase [N/s]
FW-H surface integral formulation
dB re 20 µPa
L_bb BROADBAND NOISE
Trailing Edge Broadband — Brooks-Pope-Marcolini
Turbulent boundary layer noise from blade trailing edges. Continuous frequency content. Scales with M⁵ and boundary layer displacement thickness. Dominant at high frequencies.
L_bb = 10·log₁₀(ρ₀²·c₀³·c·δ*·M⁵ / r²) + 128
δ* = 0.037·c·Re^(−0.2) [BL thickness]
Re = ρ₀·V_tip·c / μ_air
Peak freq: f_pk ≈ 0.8·V_tip / c
dB re 20 µPa
L_BVI BLADE-VORTEX INTERACTION
Blade–Vortex Interaction Noise
Impulsive noise when a rotor blade intersects the tip vortex shed by a preceding blade or rotor. Characteristic of tandem and coaxial configurations, and transition-phase flight. Produces strong high-amplitude tonal bursts.
L_BVI = L_load + 3·bvi_factor
bvi_factor = 8·μ (for μ > 0.05)
Coaxial enhancement: +3-6 dB inter-rotor
dB re 20 µPa
L_total COMBINED SPL
Energy Summation of Sources
Total noise is the log-energy (power) sum of all noise sources. Sources cannot be simply added linearly — only power additions are physically correct.
L_total = 10·log₁₀(Σᵢ 10^(Lᵢ/10))
Sources: thickness + loading + BVI + broadband
Ground reflection: +3 dB (coherent)
Ground absorption: −3·α [dB] correction
dB(A)
Δf_D DOPPLER SHIFT
Doppler Frequency Shift (Flyover)
Frequency shift experienced when source moves relative to observer. Causes BPF to appear higher during approach and lower during departure. Relevant for flyover certification measurements.
f_obs = f_source / (1 ∓ M_flight)
ΔL_Doppler = 20·log₁₀(1/(1−M_flight))
M_flight = V_cruise / c₀
−: approaching, +: receding
Hz shift / dB boost
STRUCTURAL ACOUSTICS & TRANSMISSION LOSS
TL TRANSMISSION LOSS
Structural Transmission Loss
Sound energy attenuation through a panel or structural element. Combined with absorption inside the cabin to determine interior noise levels. Frequency-dependent, with a coincidence dip.
TL = SPL_incident − SPL_transmitted
Mass law: TL ≈ 20·log₁₀(f·m) − 47.2
m = surface mass density [kg/m²]
dB
f_c COINCIDENCE FREQUENCY
Critical (Coincidence) Frequency
Frequency at which the acoustic wavelength matches the bending wave speed in a panel. Causes a severe dip in TL. Structural damping must compensate. Important for glass window sizing.
f_c = (c₀²/2π)·√(m/B_plate)
B_plate = E·h³/(12(1−ν²)) [bending stiffness]
E = Young's modulus, ν = Poisson ratio
Hz
α ABSORPTION COEFFICIENT
Sound Absorption Coefficient
Fraction of incident sound energy absorbed by a surface (α=0: perfect reflector, α=1: perfect absorber). Used in room acoustics and cabin interior noise prediction (sabine formula).
α(f) = 1 − |R(f)|²
R = reflection coefficient
NRC = avg of α at 250, 500, 1k, 2k Hz
0 to 1
RT₆₀ REVERBERATION TIME
Reverberation Time
Time for sound to decay by 60 dB after source stops. Used in cabin interior design — short RT₆₀ reduces reverberant build-up, lowering total interior SPL.
RT₆₀ = 0.161 · V / A
V = cabin volume [m³]
A = Σ(Sᵢ · αᵢ) = total absorption area [m²]
seconds
POROUS MEDIA MODELS (JCA & FOAM)
JCA JOHNSON-CHAMPOUX-ALLARD
Johnson–Champoux–Allard Model
Semi-phenomenological model for wave propagation in porous elastic media. Uses 5 non-acoustic parameters to predict complex effective density and bulk modulus as functions of frequency.
ρ_eff(ω) = (α∞·ρ₀/φ)·[1 + (σφ/iωα∞ρ₀)·G(ω)]
K_eff(ω) = γP₀/φ / [γ − (γ−1)/G₂(ω)]
G = viscous correction function
G₂ = thermal correction function
Pa · s/m | Pa
φ POROSITY
Open Porosity
Ratio of open pore volume to total material volume. Controls the fraction of the medium available for acoustic propagation. High porosity (φ→1) gives better acoustic coupling.
φ = V_pores / V_total
Typical open-cell foams: φ = 0.90–0.98
Melamine foam: φ ≈ 0.99
dimensionless (0–1)
σ FLOW RESISTIVITY
Airflow Resistivity
Resistance to steady airflow through the porous material per unit thickness. Primary parameter governing low-frequency absorption. Too high: acoustic waves reflected. Too low: no viscous dissipation.
σ = ΔP / (u · L) [Pa·s/m²]
ΔP = pressure drop, u = flow velocity
Optimum: σ ≈ ρ₀c₀/L for max absorption
Typical range: 1,000–50,000 Pa·s/m²
Pa·s/m²
α∞ TORTUOSITY
Tortuosity (High-Frequency Limit)
Geometrical parameter describing how tortuous (winding) the pore paths are. Higher tortuosity increases effective acoustic path length, shifting absorption to lower frequencies.
α∞ = (c_eff/c₀)² ≥ 1
Straight pores: α∞ = 1
Foam typical: α∞ = 1.1–2.5
Controls high-freq phase velocity
dimensionless (≥1)
Λ, Λ' CHARACTERISTIC LENGTHS
Viscous & Thermal Characteristic Lengths
Micro-structural length scales governing the transition between viscous (low-f) and inertial (high-f) behaviour. Λ governs viscous dissipation; Λ' governs thermal exchange with pore walls.
Λ: viscous characteristic length [µm]
Λ': thermal characteristic length [µm]
Λ'/Λ ≈ 2–3 for most foams
Transition freq ≈ σφ/(πρ₀α∞Λ²)
µm
HELMHOLTZ RESONATORS
f₀ RESONANCE FREQUENCY
Helmholtz Resonator Frequency
A cavity connected to a room through a narrow neck acts as a spring-mass resonator. The neck air mass oscillates against the cavity spring (compressible air), absorbing energy at the tuned frequency.
f₀ = (c₀/2π) · √(S_p / (L_eff · V_cav))
S_p = neck (hole) cross-section [m²]
L_eff = t + 2δ (effective neck length)
δ = end correction ≈ 0.85·r_hole
V_cav = cavity volume [m³]
Hz
Q QUALITY FACTOR
Resonator Quality Factor
Ratio of resonator frequency to bandwidth. High Q = narrow, deep absorption peak. Low Q = broad, shallower absorption. Viscous losses in the neck control Q. Arrays of resonators with spread f₀ give broadband performance.
Q = f₀ / Δf₃dB
Peak absorption α_max → 1 at resonance
for critically matched impedance: Z = ρ₀c₀
dimensionless

FREQUENCY-DOMAIN STRATEGY

LOW FREQUENCY (63–250 Hz)
BPF tones dominate. Mass law TL is limited. Helmholtz resonators tuned to BPF harmonics provide targeted absorption. Heavy panels and constrained-layer damping help.
MID FREQUENCY (250 Hz–2 kHz)
Porous foam (JCA) is most effective here. Absorption rises with frequency. Thickness and flow resistivity optimised for this range. Peak performance at 500–1000 Hz.
HIGH FREQUENCY (2–8 kHz)
Broadband rotor noise. Glass TL is excellent here — coincidence must be avoided above 4 kHz. Thin absorptive facings on panels clean up residual noise.

RECOMMENDED ACOUSTIC PACKAGE

Integrated eVTOL Cabin Treatment Stack (inner to outer):

1. Perforated liner panel — 2mm aluminium/composite, 8mm holes @ 30mm c/c → Helmholtz resonator array tuned to 1st and 2nd BPF harmonic
2. Air cavity — 60–80mm, optionally filled with 25mm melamine foam (JCA: φ=0.99, σ=8kPa·s/m²) for broadband damping
3. Structural fuselage skin — mass adds to transmission loss (TL increases ~6 dB per doubling of surface mass)
4. Constrained-layer damping tape on structural panels where panel resonances coincide with BPF
5. Windows — 6mm laminated glass (PVB interlayer) minimum; avoid single-pane above 200cm²; use double pane if cabin sound target <65 dB(A)
6. Interior trim — minimum 15mm open-cell foam behind fabric: NRC ≥ 0.7 to reduce reverberation and interior reflections

Expected combined reduction: 15–25 dB(A) across the BPF spectrum, achieving ICAO-compatible interior levels.
